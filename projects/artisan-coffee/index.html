Our Story

Terra Firma was born out of a profound respect for the earth and the incredible journey a coffee bean takes from seed to cup. Founded by a group of passionate coffee professionals who traded the bustling city life for the serene, inspiring landscapes of Goa, we set out to create a roastery that honors both the producer and the consumer.

We believe that exceptional coffee is not just about the roast; it's about the relationships we build with farmers across the globe. From the high altitudes of the Ethiopian highlands to the volcanic soils of Guatemala, and right here in the lush estates of Chikmagalur, we source beans that are cultivated with care and sustainability in mind.

Our roasting philosophy is simple: highlight the intrinsic qualities of each lot. We don't mask the flavors with heavy roasting; instead, we carefully develop the profile to bring out the unique terroir, varietal characteristics, and processing methods. Every batch is a testament to our dedication to quality, transparency, and the vibrant community we are building here in Goa.

When you drink Terra Firma, you are experiencing the culmination of thousands of hours of hard work by farmers, pickers, millers, and roasters. We invite you to join us on this journey of exploration and appreciation for specialty coffee.

The Art & Science of Roasting

At Terra Firma, roasting is where science meets intuition. We utilize a state-of-the-art Loring Smart Roaster, which employs a closed-loop roasting environment. This not only drastically reduces our carbon footprint by eliminating the need for an afterburner, but it also provides unparalleled consistency and control over the roasting environment.

Every new coffee that arrives at our facility undergoes rigorous sample roasting and cupping. We develop multiple roast profiles, tweaking variables such as charge temperature, rate of rise, and development time. Our goal is to find the "sweet spot" where the coffee's inherent acidity, sweetness, and body are in perfect harmony.

We log every roast using advanced software to ensure that once a profile is locked in, it can be replicated with exacting precision. However, we never rely solely on technology. Our head roaster relies on sensory cues—the smell of the Maillard reaction, the sound of first crack, and the color of the beans—to make micro-adjustments on the fly.

Quality control is paramount. Every production batch is cupped the following day to verify that it meets our stringent standards before it is packaged and shipped to you. This relentless pursuit of perfection is what defines the Terra Firma experience.

Brew Guides

Brewing great coffee at home is a rewarding ritual. While there are endless variables to tweak, these baseline recipes will help you extract the best flavors from our beans. Remember, the best cup of coffee is the one you enjoy, so feel free to experiment!

Hario V60 Pour Over

The V60 is renowned for producing a clean, bright cup that highlights the nuanced flavors and acidity of specialty coffee. It requires a gooseneck kettle for precise pouring.

Ratio: 1:15 (20g coffee to 300g water)
Grind: Medium-fine (like table salt)
Water Temp: 93°C - 96°C

  1. Place the filter in the V60 and rinse thoroughly with hot water to remove any papery taste and preheat the brewer. Discard the rinse water.
  2. Add 20g of ground coffee, shake gently to level the bed, and tare your scale.
  3. 0:00 - The Bloom: Pour 60g of water in a circular motion, ensuring all grounds are saturated. Wait 45 seconds. This allows trapped CO2 to escape.
  4. 0:45 - First Pour: Slowly pour in concentric circles until the scale reads 150g.
  5. 1:15 - Second Pour: Continue pouring gently until you reach 225g.
  6. 1:45 - Final Pour: Pour the remaining water to reach 300g. Give the V60 a gentle swirl to settle the bed flat.
  7. The drawdown should finish around 2:45 to 3:15. If it drains too fast, grind finer next time; if too slow, grind coarser.

AeroPress (Inverted Method)

The AeroPress is incredibly versatile and forgiving. The inverted method allows for full immersion brewing, resulting in a full-bodied cup with rich flavors.

Ratio: 1:14 (15g coffee to 210g water)
Grind: Medium-fine
Water Temp: 90°C - 93°C

  1. Push the plunger just inside the brewing chamber and stand it upside down on the plunger.
  2. Place a paper filter in the cap and rinse it with hot water.
  3. Add 15g of coffee into the chamber.
  4. 0:00: Pour 50g of water and stir vigorously to ensure all grounds are wet. Let it bloom for 30 seconds.
  5. 0:30: Pour the remaining water up to 210g. Stir gently once or twice.
  6. 1:30: Screw the rinsed filter cap tightly onto the chamber.
  7. 1:45: Carefully flip the entire assembly onto a sturdy mug.
  8. Press down slowly and steadily. The plunge should take about 30 seconds. Stop when you hear a hissing sound.

French Press

The classic immersion brewer. It produces a heavy, textured cup because the metal mesh filter allows coffee oils and fine particles to pass through.

Ratio: 1:15 (30g coffee to 450g water)
Grind: Coarse (like sea salt)
Water Temp: 95°C

  1. Preheat the French Press glass carafe with hot water, then discard.
  2. Add 30g of coarsely ground coffee.
  3. 0:00: Pour 450g of hot water vigorously to saturate all the grounds. Start your timer.
  4. 4:00: A crust of coffee grounds will have formed at the top. Take a spoon and gently break the crust, stirring a few times. Most of the grounds will fall to the bottom.
  5. Using two spoons, skim off the remaining foam and floating bits from the surface. This step significantly cleans up the final cup.
  6. 5:00: Place the plunger on top but do not press it all the way down. Just press it just below the surface of the liquid.
  7. Wait another 3-5 minutes. This allows the fine particles to settle at the bottom.
  8. Pour gently into your mug, leaving the last bit of liquid in the press to avoid sludge.

Espresso

Espresso is a concentrated beverage brewed by forcing hot water under pressure through finely-ground coffee. It requires an espresso machine and a high-quality burr grinder.

Ratio: 1:2 to 1:2.5 (18g in, 36g - 45g out)
Grind: Very fine
Water Temp: 93°C

  1. Remove the portafilter, wipe the basket clean and dry.
  2. Grind 18g of coffee into the basket.
  3. Distribute the grounds evenly using a distribution tool or by tapping the sides of the portafilter.
  4. Tamp the coffee bed firmly and perfectly level. The pressure should be around 15kg, but consistency is more important than absolute force.
  5. Flush the group head of your machine for a few seconds to stabilize temperature and clean the shower screen.
  6. Insert the portafilter, place your cup and scale underneath, tare, and start the extraction immediately.
  7. Aim for a yield of 36g to 45g of liquid espresso in about 25 to 30 seconds.
  8. If the shot runs too fast (under 20s), grind finer. If it runs too slow (over 35s), grind coarser. Adjust to taste!

Our Commitment to the Earth

At Terra Firma, sustainability is not a buzzword; it is woven into the very fabric of our business model. We recognize that coffee agriculture is highly vulnerable to climate change, and we have a responsibility to mitigate our impact.

We practice direct trade whenever possible, ensuring that the farmers who grow our coffee receive a premium price well above the commodity market rate. This financial stability allows them to invest in sustainable farming practices, pay fair wages to their workers, and support their local communities.

In our roastery in Goa, we have implemented a zero-waste initiative. All our coffee chaff and organic waste are composted and distributed to local community gardens. Our retail packaging is 100% compostable, made from plant-based materials that break down harmlessly in a home compost bin.

By choosing Terra Firma, you are not just buying a bag of coffee; you are supporting a supply chain that values environmental stewardship and human dignity. Together, we can ensure that great coffee remains sustainable for generations to come.
